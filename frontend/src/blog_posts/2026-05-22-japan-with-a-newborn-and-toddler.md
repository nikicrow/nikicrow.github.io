I'm writing this from Japan. We're a month into what has turned out to be a bold but great mat leave decision, and I wanted to write it down while it's all fresh — both to share and because I'll definitely want to read this back one day.

Some context: I'm half Japanese and lived here with my husband Ben for a year and a half back in 2019, so this isn't completely uncharted territory for me. But this trip is a very different beast. I now have two kids: Ember, who is two and a half, and Imogen (Immy), who is two months old. We're here as a family of four, plus family coming in and out.

And honestly? It's been lovely. Japan is a surprisingly good place to do mat leave, and I want to explain why — and what I'd tell anyone considering something similar.

---

## Why Japan is awesome for mat leave

The exchange rate right now is _insane_ for Australians. Like, amazingly good. That alone makes a month here feel like a pretty smart financial decision.

Then there's the konbini situation. If you've never been to Japan, you need to understand that the 7-Elevens and Lawsons and FamilyMarts here are nothing like the ones back home. They have hot microwave-ready meals, fresh onigiri, actual dinners. Between the konbinis and the supermarkets, the idea of cooking a proper meal feels like why would you. When you're sleep-deprived and running on fumes, being able to walk three minutes and come back with dinner is not a small thing.

We're in an Airbnb that includes cleaning. I cannot tell you how much that matters when you have a newborn. It's also genuinely affordable here right now, which — see: exchange rate.

I also have family here, which makes everything easier. Familiar faces, local knowledge, people who just show up and hold the baby for a bit. If you have any connection like that, absolutely lean into it.

But the biggest thing? We're here for a **month**. Not a week. Not ten days trying to cram in every temple and ramen shop and famous neighbourhood. We're just... living here for a while. That pace shift is everything when you have small kids. You can have a bad day. You can stay in. You don't have to achieve anything. That's mat leave done right, honestly.

---

## Tips for actually doing this

These are the things that have made the trip work for us.

### Don't cheap out on flights

We flew business class and chose a direct flight. I know that's not always possible, but if it is, do it. Bigger seats, more space, and — crucially — you're not standing in a long economy check-in queue with a newborn and a wriggly toddler before you've even left the country. When you have two young kids in tow, every bit of friction you remove from travel day is worth it.

### Stay in one place — and choose carefully

We're not moving around. One Airbnb, one neighbourhood, the whole month. I can't overstate how much this matters.

And the location is everything. You want to be within easy walking distance of a konbini, a proper supermarket, and a drugstore. That's it. Those three things. If you have those, you can handle most of what gets thrown at you. Being able to pop out with just Ember, or just Immy — not necessarily both at the same time — is what makes the daily logistics feel manageable rather than impossible.

### Make sure remote work is an option

Ben has been working from Japan this whole trip. Having that option is what made it possible to stay for a month rather than racing back after two weeks. We're not rushing, not cramming, not stressed about maximising every single day. That slower pace is necessary when you have young kids. If your partner can work remotely, it's worth building the whole trip around it.

### Don't expect amazing playgrounds

I want to set realistic expectations here because I had slightly rose-tinted memories of Japan from before we had kids. The parks and playgrounds we've found aren't necessarily amazing for toddlers — at least not in the areas we've been. There are probably good ones somewhere but not where we stayed...

What's been gold is having outdoor space at the accommodation itself. Our Airbnb has a small rock garden. Ember _loves_ it. She will spend a surprisingly long time out there just messing around with rocks and sticks and whatever else she finds. That little patch of outside has saved us countless times from having to pack everyone up and actually leave the house when we didn't have the energy for it. I just bought bubble solution every few days.

### Uber Eats is a lifesaver

I mean this in the most literal way. It's super easy, super cheap, and the delivery is fast because everything is so close together. When you're running on broken sleep and the baby has been feeding all evening and you've got nothing left for dinner — two taps and food arrives. We've used it for multiple meals already and will keep doing so.

---

## Would I recommend it?

Absolutely. Japan suits this season of life in a way I didn't fully expect, even though I knew it would work. The food situation is easy, the infrastructure is great, the cost is manageable, and staying for a long stretch rather than doing a frantic holiday makes it genuinely restful in the ways mat leave is supposed to be restful.

If you're Australian, have any connection to Japan, and are wondering whether to take the leap — do it.
