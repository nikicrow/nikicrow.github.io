I want to tell you about Data Divas. Not just what it is, but how it actually came to be — because the origin story matters.

## How It Started

There was a restructure at TPG. During it, the only woman at head-of-level in our department was made redundant.

HR pulled the women in the data department into a room. The question: "Are you okay?"

The honest answer that came back: not really. There were no groups, no resources, no community for women in data at TPG. No networking, no mentoring, no sense that there was something to tap into when things got hard or when you wanted to grow.

HR asked if anyone wanted to do something about it.

I put my hand up.

I'd gotten a lot from Women in STEM and Women in Tech programs over the years — the kind of thing where you walk away feeling less alone and more capable. I wanted that to exist inside the org. My colleague **Biji George** also volunteered, and honestly, I don't think any of this would have happened without her.

## Building the Thing

Biji and I started developing ideas for what a Women in Data group at TPG could look like. We went back to the department to ideate — on the name, on the mission, on what we actually wanted this to be.

The name "Data Divas" came from that process. And yes — you don't have to be a lady to be a diva. Anyone who champions women in data is welcome.

Then we reached out to AWS and Databricks. Both said yes. The AWS relationship in particular really stuck, and a string of incredible events followed.

We also had strong backing from our leadership team — **Ben Thomas, Michael Gumbley, Pedram Keshavarzian, Scott Young, and Christopher Michael** — and I'm grateful for it. You can put your hand up all you want, but without leadership support, grassroots programs die quietly. This one didn't.

## 2025: The Year That Happened

We went from zero to 80+ members in under a year. Here's what that actually looked like.

### The Launch

We launched in February with a bang. **Kieren Cooney** (Group Executive Consumer) joined us, alongside panelists **Biji George**, **Christopher S.**, and an external voice, **Viv**. It set the tone — this wasn't going to be a box-ticking diversity exercise. It was going to be real conversations with real people.

### AWS Partnership Events

Our AWS partnership delivered two standout sessions. **Tatjana Pandurevic** spoke on AI at TPG, and **Caroline Clerc (Lamaison)** brought an AI strategy lens. These were the kinds of conversations you don't usually get inside the walls of a single org.

### Databricks Event

We hosted a career journeys event with two Databricks speakers alongside TPG's own **Danielle A.**, hosted by the brilliant **Jessica McGregor**. I love these format — career journeys are honest in a way that polished keynotes aren't.

### Amazon Fulfillment Centre Excursion

This one was a highlight. We got a tour of Amazon's Fulfillment Centre — automation, innovation culture, the works. **Celine Wang** and **Sam Haskins** made it happen, and it was genuinely eye-opening.

### Fireside Chats (Standing Room Only)

We ran two fireside chats. Both were so popular people had to stand. **AWS speakers**, TPG's **CMO Rebecca Darley**, and **Executive GM Melanie Van De Merwe** joined us, hosted by **Stephanie Moll**. The energy in those rooms was something else.

### Analytics CoP Collab

We partnered with the Analytics Community of Practice — shoutout to **Pabs Tuladhar** and AWS — for a joint session that brought the two communities together.

### Moving into OneTech + STEMPower

We expanded our footprint by moving into the OneTech community and connecting with **STEMPower** (thank you, **Madeleine Gillard**). Getting connected to a broader ecosystem felt like levelling up.

### The Mentoring Program

Okay, this is my favourite thing we did all year, and I'll tell you why.

We ran a structured mentoring program: **18 mentees, 18 mentors**. We built out mentor training, created session guidelines, and closed it out with an end-of-year lunch.

There's something about a mentoring program that feels different to an event. Events are great — you leave energised, you meet people, you get ideas. But a mentoring program is a relationship. It's a commitment. And when it works, it genuinely changes people's trajectories.

That's what this was built for.

## What I've Learned

Starting something like this from scratch inside a large organisation is harder than it looks from the outside. You're not just organising events — you're building trust, navigating org politics, getting speaker confirmations that fall through at the last minute, and trying to create something that feels genuine when so much corporate "diversity" stuff doesn't.

The thing that kept it real was the community itself. The people who showed up, the members who referred colleagues, the partners who brought their best speakers. We built this together.
