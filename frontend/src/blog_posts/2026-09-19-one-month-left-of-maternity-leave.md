In almost exactly a month, I'm going back to work.

This isn't my first time doing this. I went back to work after having Ember in 2023, but somehow this time feels quite different.

## How it went last time

With Ember, I had about five and a half, almost six months off before I went back part-time. I had an arrangement where I could work three days a week for 12 weeks and still be paid fully, which was honestly a pretty amazing way to ease back into things.

Although, in reality, it wasn't quite that straightforward. About two months in, two of my colleagues went on holiday and I ended up working full-time for a month to cover them. Then I went back to part-time for another month, then full-time again, and not long after that I changed jobs.

I remember being really anxious about going back.

But I also remember being ready.

I loved being with Ember, and I felt incredibly sad about not being there for her all the time, but I was also genuinely bored. There was a lot more time when it was just the baby and me at home, and while there was something lovely and quiet about that, I really missed intellectual stimulation. I missed using that part of my brain.

Going back part-time felt manageable because I was fully remote and Mum and my mother-in-law were looking after Ember at our house. I could be working in another room, come out and see her, continue breastfeeding, and avoid the whole logistical nightmare of pumping enough milk to get through a workday.

## What I wanted to do differently

When I thought about having another baby, though, one thing I knew I wanted to do differently was take a longer maternity leave.

And I have.

This time I'll have had closer to seven or eight months at home before I go back. And I'm really glad I did.

There's another big difference this time too: Ben is taking four months of paternity leave when I go back.

We're essentially swapping.

I go back to work and he becomes the parent at home.

That makes me feel enormously reassured. I know the kids are going to be with someone I completely trust, and because I'll be fully remote until January, I should still be able to breastfeed Imogen during the day.

## The January problem

January is the part that intimidates me more.

My work's remote-work policy has become much stricter while I've been away, and there's now an expectation of being in the office for five hours, two days a week. Exemptions aren't particularly easy to get anymore.

I genuinely don't know how that is going to fit into our life with two little kids.

Maybe it will be completely manageable. Maybe it won't. Maybe I'll eventually decide that I need to find another job.

But I don't actually have to solve January yet.

I only have to solve October.

And for the first few months, the kids will be at home with their dad, I'll be working remotely, and we'll figure out the rest as we get closer to it.

By January, Imogen will also be older. Breastfeeding might look completely different. She might only want milk in the morning and evening, or perhaps one feed during the workday. I may not need to spend half my office days attached to a breast pump just to maintain my supply and make sure she has enough milk.

## The bottle situation

At the moment, though, there is one slightly significant problem with this plan.

Imogen absolutely refuses to take a bottle.

We have tried. So many times.

She either has no interest in sucking on it or gets extremely upset that someone has had the audacity to put this strange plastic imposter anywhere near her mouth.

It hasn't helped that Ben has been really busy with work and mostly able to try feeding her in the evenings, which is probably the worst possible time to teach her.

By evening, she isn't necessarily looking to me just because she is hungry. She wants comfort. She wants Mum. And an unfamiliar bottle that she doesn't really know how to drink from is not exactly an appealing substitute.

I'm hoping that once Ben is on leave and can try during the day — when she's calm and genuinely hungry rather than tired and wanting comfort — something might click.

Once we get over that hurdle, I think I'll feel much more confident about being able to leave the house without the kids.

## What I'm actually excited about

Emotionally, though, I'm surprisingly okay about going back.

Mixed, definitely. But okay.

I'm excited to be part of something bigger than my own little bubble again.

I'm excited to use my brain.

I'm excited to see what AI has done to my workplace while I've been gone.

I'm excited to go to the bathroom whenever I want.

I'm excited to eat when I'm hungry.

And I'm weirdly excited to have my day divided into meetings and blocks of work again, instead of constantly calculating life in little pockets of 30 minutes to an hour between feeds, naps, preschool drop-offs, snacks, nappies and somebody needing something from me.

## And what I'm going to miss

But I'm also really sad that this part is ending.

This maternity leave has felt different. We've spent more time with friends. We've gone to parks. We've had ordinary weekdays together that we probably wouldn't have had otherwise.

And Imogen is at such a gorgeous age now.

She's not just a little blob who wants milk anymore.

She's a person.

She has opinions. She likes things. She dislikes things. She gets excited. She recognises people. She has this little personality that seems to be appearing more every day.

And right now, she really adores me.

There's something bittersweet about leaving maternity leave at exactly the point where your baby becomes so much fun to hang out with.

I wonder how she'll feel about having a little less of me.

I wonder how I'll feel about having a little less of her.

---

But strangely, I don't feel like I need to have the whole thing figured out before I go back.

I trust Ben with the kids. I trust that we'll work out the feeding. I trust that we'll adapt when January comes. And if I go back to work and discover that the arrangement simply doesn't work for our family anymore, then we'll figure out what comes next.

For now, I have one month left.

One more month of parks and naps and feeds and weekday catch-ups. One more month where my calendar is mostly dictated by a tiny person's sleep schedule.

And then, for a while at least, it's Dad's turn.

I think I'm ready.

And I'm also really, really going to miss this.
